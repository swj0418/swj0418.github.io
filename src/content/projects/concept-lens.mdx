## The problem

Latent directions in generative models promise controllable edits — *add spots*, *close the eyes* — but a direction rarely does the same thing to every image. An edit that works cleanly on one group of inputs can be entangled with something else, or do nothing at all, on another. Evaluating a manipulation method means seeing both sides at once: the edits and the inputs they're applied to.

## How it works

Concept Lens pairs two hierarchies with an image grid:

- **Concepts** — edit directions, clustered by the changes they produce.
- **Codes** — latent inputs, clustered by what they generate.
- **A grid** of generated images for any selected codes (rows) × concepts (columns).

Hierarchy nodes encode the **consistency** and **magnitude** of each edit, so directions that act uniformly stand apart from ones whose effect depends on the input. Brushing a subset of codes and re-clustering the concept hierarchy surfaces edits that are consistent for that subset.

## An example

A *closed eyes* concept applies to most cat images — except a group of kittens, which in the dataset rarely have their eyes closed. For another group of cats, the same direction is entangled with *mouth open*. These are the kinds of input-dependent behaviors that aggregate metrics hide.

The TVCG version broadens the system to compare and evaluate manipulations produced by different direction-finding methods — SeFA, GANSpace, vector arithmetic, and supervised (SVM) directions.
